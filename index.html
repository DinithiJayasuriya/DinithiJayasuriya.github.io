// 01 — about

Bridging machine learning and chip design

I'm a PhD Research Assistant at the University of Illinois Chicago, working at the intersection of EDA and AI. My research studies reliability and uncertainty in LLM-assisted hardware-design workflows (OpenROAD) through controlled experiments, failure analysis, and metric-driven optimization.

Before my PhD, I spent two years at Synopsys as an Application Engineer in Clock Domain Crossing verification — debugging complex customer issues, owning end-to-end enablement for Apple and Renesas, and building regression-style validation workflows. I validated a glitch-identification feature for Apple to 98% accuracy and earned an internal performance scholarship.

My strengths sit where rigorous hardware engineering meets applied ML: RTL & verification fundamentals, SPICE-based analog evaluation, physical-design PPA, and scripting-driven experiment pipelines — now amplified with causal inference and agentic LLM tooling.

// 02 — research focus

What I work on

Three threads connecting reliable AI systems with the realities of silicon and circuit design.

🤖

LLM-Assisted EDA

Verifier-guided code generation for OpenROAD that enforces structural correctness before execution — cutting reliance on tool-in-the-loop debugging and unreliable candidates.

🔀

Causal Circuit Optimization

Learning the causal structure of analog circuits to make multi-objective optimization interpretable, sample-efficient, and transferable across technologies — validated in SPICE.

🔬

Verification & Reliability

RTL/CDC verification rigor, design-of-experiments, and failure analysis — building reproducible pipelines that quantify PPA and quality-of-results trade-offs.
